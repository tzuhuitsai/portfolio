Overview

Artful Cause is a platform where people with creative talents can offer their services to non-profit organizations, while organizations can discover and connect with skilled volunteers. It runs as both a dedicated mobile app and a responsive website.

I handled the full design process from user research through to high-fidelity prototype: interviews, personas, competitive audit, Crazy 8 ideation, wireframes, lo-fi and hi-fi prototypes, usability testing, and responsive design.

The Problem

Many designers and artists want to contribute to social causes — but struggle to find relevant opportunities. On the other side, non-profit organizations have limited budgets and can't always access the design talent they need. Neither side has a dedicated place to find each other.

Research & Ideation

I conducted user interviews with a range of potential users to understand motivations, frustrations, and expectations. Empathy maps and personas captured two distinct user types.

Persona — Sarah Chen

A 28-year-old freelance photographer who wants to contribute her skills to non-profit work, but finds it time-consuming to search across different platforms for the right opportunities.

Persona — David Thompson

A professional photographer with a Master's degree who wants to find non-profit projects aligned with his values. He faces challenges vetting organizations and coordinating project details, and prefers working from desktop or tablet.

Competitive audit

I audited platforms that match volunteers and talent to projects, examining their strengths, weaknesses, and feature gaps — particularly around profile presentation and project browsing.

Key Finding

No existing platform combined a volunteer-specific profile showcase with cause-based matching. The gap was clear — and the opportunity for differentiation was in giving volunteers a place to express both their skills and their values.

Ideation — Crazy 8

I used the Crazy 8 method to rapidly explore layout and flow possibilities before committing to any direction.

Key Design Decisions
Profile preview page separate from edit mode
Showing volunteers exactly how their profile appears to others — separate from the edit state — helps them make intentional presentation decisions rather than guessing how their edits will look in context.
Contact information prominently surfaced on profile
Unlike generic platforms, the primary goal for profile visitors is to initiate contact. Making contact info always visible — not buried — reduces the steps between discovery and connection.
Direct project editing from the profile page (usability study fix)
Usability testing revealed that the original two-step path (edit profile → then edit projects via a separate entry point) was confusing. Bringing project editing directly to the profile page made the flow more intuitive and reduced unnecessary navigation.

Takeaways
①

The same platform can have a different primary purpose depending on whether users are on the app or the website. Designing both requires understanding the context of each — not just adapting layouts.

②

Usability testing — even with just 3 participants — surfaces real friction that assumptions miss. The editing flow fix came entirely from watching users navigate, not from design intuition.

③

Designing for a two-sided marketplace means understanding two distinct mental models simultaneously. Keeping both users' goals visible throughout the design process prevents accidentally optimizing for one at the other's expense.
